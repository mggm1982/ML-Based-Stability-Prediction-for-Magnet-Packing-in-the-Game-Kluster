# Limitations

This study evaluates machine learning screening models against a simplified numerical physics simulator. The following scope and modelling boundaries apply:

1. **Point-Dipole Idealisation:** Magnets are modelled as idealised vertical point dipoles in two dimensions. Finite-size multipole corrections are not computed, and forces become less accurate near contact ($r \to D$). No specific error percentage is assumed or claimed for the point-dipole approximation.
2. **Constrained Fixed-Vertical Moments:** Dipole moments are strictly constrained to vertical orientations ($+z$ or $-z$). Real magnetic stones experience three-dimensional torques that cause tipping, rocking, or flipping over their edges before sliding.
3. **Absence of 3D Motion and Rotation:** Magnets are modelled as rigid flat discs undergoing pure planar translation with zero rotation or tumbling.
4. **Idealised Boundary:** The confining boundary is treated as a rigid, circular, frictionless, and perfectly inelastic wall, rather than the flexible cord used in the commercial tabletop game Kluster.
5. **Irregular Geometry:** Commercial hematite/neodymium stones possess irregular, non-cylindrical, smoothed pebble geometries, unlike the uniform identical discs modelled here.
6. **Simulation-Derived Labels:** All ground-truth labels (`contact`, `settled`, `timeout`, `unresolved`) are generated by a physics-based numerical model using illustrative nominal parameters. Agreement with the numerical model does not constitute agreement with real physical magnets.
7. **Finite Observation Horizon:** The simulation horizon is fixed at $T = 1.0\text{ s}$. `timeout` denotes lack of equilibrium within 1 second and is not a proof of infinite-time instability or eventual equilibrium.
8. **Illustrative Parameter Values:** Nominal physical constants (remanence $B_r = 1.3\text{ T}$, density $\rho = 7500\text{ kg/m}^3$, friction coefficients $\mu_s = 0.30$, $\mu_k = 0.20$) are illustrative and have not been empirically calibrated to specific commercial game pieces.
9. **Synthetic Placement Process:** Candidates are placed by uniform spatial sampling across valid disc positions rather than human tactical play.
10. **Absence of Safety Certification:** Statistical risk bounds derived via Conformal Risk Control guarantee bounded error rates only with respect to the numerical simulator under exchangeable data splits; they do not provide physical safety certification for human-operated systems.
